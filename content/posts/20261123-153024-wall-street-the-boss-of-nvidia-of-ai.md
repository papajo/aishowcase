### Eleven days out, then back in

On Monday, November 23, Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, president and board member, effective immediately. That came 11 days after he resigned following revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman U.S. officials suspect of spying for China. Only last week, on the earnings call, the board said it did not intend to bring him back.

The board says it reversed course after "input from senior levels of the United States government," which left it confident his return poses no national security risk. President Trump publicly welcomed the decision the same morning. The investigation into the foreign-intelligence reporting continues. Huang is due for a closed-door interview with three House committees on December 1 and has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee.

### The market voted first

In early trading Nvidia stock jumped more than 30%, its biggest move in a decade. It had lost about a third of its value in the two weeks Huang was gone. The Nasdaq rallied about 6% and the S&P 500 about 4%, as investors who had dumped AI shares on Friday piled back in.

One strategist at Interactive Brokers summed up the mood: Huang looked "too big to be fired." You can't know how much the administration or shareholders pushed. But the sequence speaks for itself: resignation accepted, "no intention to reinstate," a third of the market cap gone, then a unanimous reversal.

### Why this matters if you build with AI

1. **Wall Street sets Nvidia's terms.** A board that had already accepted a resignation reversed itself within days once the stock and the wider AI trade started falling. That's what having a market valuation large enough to move whole indexes looks like.
2. **Nvidia sets the AI industry's terms.** One company's CEO question pulled down AI-exposed stocks across the market. Most training stacks still run on Nvidia hardware and CUDA. Nvidia's governance risk is the whole industry's supply risk.
3. **Single-vendor dependence is a real engineering risk.** If your product only runs on one vendor's GPUs, a leadership crisis there is your crisis too. Keeping your code portable, by testing on more than one backend, choosing open model formats and keeping a fallback provider, is cheap insurance.

### What to watch

- The ongoing investigation, and what comes out of the December 1 House interview and the Senate testimony.
- Whether the board explains its process. Analysts have already asked how it could push him out within 24 hours and then bring him back days later.
- Whether the AI trade's rebound holds once the relief rally fades.

The headline writes itself: Wall Street is the boss of Nvidia, and Nvidia is the boss of AI. This week showed how short that chain of command really is.
